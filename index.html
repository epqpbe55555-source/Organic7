The chemistry of Ar–OH — acidity, aromatic substitution, and the named reactions that build phenolic compounds.

The essentials

No lecture — just the rules that actually get tested.

Definition

  • General formula Ar–OH: an –OH group bonded directly to an aromatic carbon.
  • Alcohols have –OH on an aliphatic carbon — same functional group, very different chemistry.
  • Common members: phenol, cresols, catechol, resorcinol, hydroquinone, salicylic acid.

Physical properties

  • Liquids or low-melting solids; high boiling points from H-bonding.
  • Phenol: ~9 g soluble per 100 g water; most other phenols ≈ insoluble.
  • Colorless pure — color usually means oxidation products.
  • Easily oxidized; store/purify carefully.
  • o-Nitrophenol is steam-volatile, lower b.p. & lower water-solubility than its isomers.

Acidity order

Carboxylic acid> Phenol> Water> Alcohol
  • Phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilized over the ring — alkoxide is not.
  • Carboxylate is stabilized by two equivalent resonance oxygens — stronger acid than phenol.
  • Phenol is acidic enough to react with NaOH (alcohols are not).

Directing effect

  • –OH is a strong activating, ortho/para-directing group in EAS.
  • Lone pair on O donates into the ring by resonance, raising electron density at o/p.
  • Excess reagent (e.g. conc. HNO₃) → polysubstitution (picric acid).

Key named reactions — one line each

KolbeSodium phenoxide + CO₂, 125 °C / 4–7 atm → salicylic acid (ortho-COOH).
Reimer–TiemannPhenol + CHCl₃ + NaOH, 70 °C, via dichlorocarbene → salicylaldehyde (ortho-CHO).
FriesPhenolic ester + AlCl₃, heat → migrates acyl group to ring (o/p-hydroxyketone).
ClaisenAllyl phenyl ether, 200 °C, [3,3]-sigmatropic → o-allylphenol.
WilliamsonArO⁻ + R–X → Ar–O–R. R–X must be primary or methyl.
NitrosationPhenol + HONO → 4-nitrosophenol → oxidized [O] → 4-nitrophenol.
